Tech Startup Project — AI-Based Aerodynamic Geometries Generation Platform · Role: Project Lead

AI-Driven Aerodynamic Design & CFD Validation Platform

A generative-AI platform for aerodynamic design: an inverse neural model proposes airfoil and control-surface geometries from target aerodynamic requirements, automated high-fidelity CFD pipelines validate them against those targets, and an adjoint-based fine-tuning architecture is designed to feed the flow solver's sensitivities directly back into the network — closing the AI–CFD loop for wings and control surfaces.

Confidentiality notice. This project was developed in a tech-startup context involving external aerospace stakeholders. The identity of the industrial partner, the full technical dossier and quantitative results are subject to confidentiality and cannot be published. The architecture, methodology and roadmap below are described at a level of detail compatible with those obligations.

Overview

The aerodynamic design of wings and control surfaces still relies on a manually driven iteration loop between geometry definition and CFD verification. The project was built on a different workflow: a generative model proposes geometries already consistent with the target aerodynamic coefficients, and CFD is concentrated where it creates value — verification, validation and physical refinement.

As Project Lead, I owned the CFD and physical-modelling side of the platform end-to-end: solver configuration, automated validation pipelines, aerodynamic evaluation of every generated geometry, and the design of the physics-informed fine-tuning strategy. The role involved direct technical discussions with leading aerospace players.

Starting Point: Feasibility Proven, Physics Missing

The starting prototype had already demonstrated the core feasibility: a supervised inverse model learning the map from aerodynamic parameters and polar data to a 2D airfoil, with a panel method as a fast validator. As a demonstrator it was solid; for any industrial domain it carried three structural limits:

  • A physically blind validator. Panel methods assume incompressible, inviscid flow: no compressibility, no shock waves, no viscous drag — acceptable for subsonic screening, insufficient where the real operating regime lives.
  • A subsonic, strictly 2D domain. Training data and generated geometry were both confined to 2D sections at low Mach numbers.
  • An open loop. Nothing fed what validation learned back into the model: generation and physics never exchanged information.

Platform Architecture

The platform was structured in eight layers, each replaceable without touching the others — the precondition for evolving from prototype to industrial tool:

  • Data layer — airfoil geometries, polar data, operating conditions and CFD results, normalised for training, benchmarking and retraining.
  • Geometric representation — continuous parametrisations (PARSEC, CST, Bézier/B-spline, FFD) chosen for smoothness, constraint control and mesh compatibility.
  • Inverse neural model — the core: aerodynamic requirements → candidate geometry, with alternative variants and a confidence score.
  • CFD validation layer — automated case generation, meshing, solve, coefficient extraction and convergence tracking.
  • Physical fine-tuning layer — gradient-free baseline with an adjoint-based evolution path.
  • 3D geometry generation — section stacking with twist, sweep and taper, evolving towards direct 3D generation.
  • Frontend / API layer — a web application orchestrating generation, parametric editing, exports and CFD runs.
  • Experiment tracking — every run registered with its model, dataset, solver and mesh configuration: reproducible, auditable output.

Solver Chain: From Incompressible Baseline to Shock-Capturing

The validation chain was configured on two physical tiers. The incompressible solver covers the fast tier: screening candidates and supporting the training-data pipeline where compressibility effects are negligible. The compressible tier is where physical fidelity becomes non-negotiable: variable density, pressure–density–temperature coupling and the energy equation, conservative formulations of the governing equations, and shock-capturing numerical schemes with Riemann-solver-based fluxes to handle shock waves and the steep pressure gradients of transonic and supersonic regimes, with RANS turbulence models consistent with the operating regime and the wall conditions.

The panel method was deliberately demoted, not deleted: it remains a preliminary screening validator for quick 2D subsonic tests, never the final word on any geometry. This division of labour — cheap screening, expensive verification — keeps the loop fast without compromising the physics.

Automated CFD Pipelines

The automated CFD pipelines take any geometry the model generates through the entire chain unattended: computational domain, mesh generation, boundary conditions, solve, extraction of lift, drag and moment coefficients and of pressure distributions, and convergence checks. Every outcome is registered into the dataset — including non-convergent or physically implausible geometries, logged as such. Treating failures as data makes the AI–CFD pipeline more robust at every iteration, instead of silently biasing the training set towards the cases that happen to converge.

Physics-Informed Fine-Tuning: Black-Box Now, Adjoint Next

Physical fine-tuning is the layer that turns the platform from a generator into a learning system: it closes the loop between what the flow solver measures and what the network generates. The implemented baseline is gradient-free — the model is fine-tuned on the validation outcomes registered by the pipelines, failures included. The designed evolution path is adjoint-based: the flow solver's sensitivities are linked directly to the network's weights, replacing black-box iteration with a physically informed one.

Extending to 3D Control Surfaces

Wings are three-dimensional objects, so the architecture extends generation from 2D sections to 3D surfaces in two stages. Stage one is geometric: the inverse model keeps generating 2D sections, and a dedicated module stacks root and tip sections along the span — first along a straight stacking axis, then along a curved guide — interpolating intermediate sections and managing twist, sweep, taper and dihedral, smoothing the surface and exporting it mesh-ready for volume-mesh generation and finite-volume CFD 3D. Stage two is evolutionary: direct generation of 3D parametric geometries by the model itself, trained and refined on CFD 3D results. At the end of my contribution the proof-of-concept architecture was structurally capable of predicting and validating 3D control surfaces, while direct 3D generation remains a roadmap objective.

Results

  • Delivered a working proof-of-concept AI–CFD architecture: from target aerodynamic coefficients to a generated airfoil, through automated CFD validation — demonstrated to industry leaders.
  • Replaced simplified panel-method validation with physically robust CFD pipelines capable of capturing shock waves and high pressure gradients — the precondition for training data consistent with the real operating regime.
  • Designed the transition from gradient-free black-box fine-tuning to adjoint-based physical fine-tuning, linking CFD sensitivities directly to the neural network's weights.
  • Authored the complete technical roadmap for the platform's industrial evolution, drafted for a leading international aerospace group, with direct exposure to Embraer and the DTA aerospace technology district.
